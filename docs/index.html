One pathogen genome in, every next step out

When a new virus appears, someone has to decide which of its proteins a drug should aim at. That is expert judgement, and the evidence it rests on is thinnest exactly when the pathogen is new. This works it out from the genome's database ID alone, and shows its working at every step, so an expert does not have to take its word for it.

In plain terms

The question. A new virus turns up. Which of its proteins should a drug aim at? Normally the answer comes from a person who has read the literature and can compare the new virus with ones already understood.

It starts from one accession number, the public database ID for a genome, and nothing else. From that it works out which proteins the virus makes, ranks them by how promising a drug target each one looks, predicts one protein's 3-D shape, and checks whether a drug would have anywhere on it to grip. The shape has to be predicted because for a virus nobody has studied there is no solved structure to look at, and a predicted one is the only thing left to measure. Five steps, with no person naming the target.

The test below ran on SARS-CoV-2, with SARS-CoV-2 itself removed from everything the pipeline was allowed to look up, so it could not simply recognise the answer. It still put the real drug targets at the top, including the one an approved antiviral already hits.

From a genome to a ranked shortlist

Five steps, run live against public databases. What comes out is a list of candidate targets, ranked, with every score broken into its parts and every surface either measured on a deposited structure, computed on a predicted one, or refused with a reason.

In

The 12 it ranked, and why each one scored what it did

Nothing is fetched from an existing annotation of this genome. The sequence is translated in all six reading frames, and every stretch between stop codons of at least 60 residues is kept, overlaps in different frames included, because discarding one of two overlapping frames here would let length decide what homology should. The 12 longest are then searched against 17,512 reviewed viral proteins with SARS-CoV-2 itself deleted from that reference. Identification is tried in three tiers, cheapest first. The cheapest asks what share of an ORF's five-residue runs a reference protein also has, which settles a close relative in milliseconds and is the number the match column carries. Where that finds nothing, a remote tier groups residues by chemical type so a fainter resemblance still registers, then aligns the two sequences and reports a bit score there instead, on a scale the table names. A protein with no relative anywhere reaches hydropathy, the pattern of water-repelling stretches in its own sequence, which returns a class rather than a name and is scored so it cannot outrank a real match to a known relative.

The 20 chains it produced, and what each one scored

A mature chain is what the virus actually folds, and what a binder would be aimed at. A polyprotein above is made as one long protein and cut into pieces by the virus's own proteases, the enzymes it uses to cut itself up, and the boundaries used here are the ones annotated on the curated relative each ORF matched. Every row below names the ORF it came out of and links back to that row in the table above, so the cut is traceable in both directions. The five weights are stage 1's, unchanged. What moves is which of them are re-read for each piece. The match to a known relative is inherited from the parent ORF, measured once over the whole stretch, so chains out of one ORF carry one number between them. The structure count is recounted against each chain's own description, and the two class terms are re-read from it. A chain that is most of its parent keeps the parent's; a small piece cut out of it does not. Where the curated entry records a location for each molecule rather than one for the entry as a whole, whether the chain sits on the outside is worked out for the chain itself instead of inherited. Those are the terms that separate the pieces of one polyprotein from each other.

In

  • 20 chains, and any ORF nothing recognised along with them — this step needs a sequence and nothing else, so it is the one an unknown protein reaches

Out

  • All 20 had their own sequence located in this genome, by alignment rather than by trusting the relative's numbering

  • Every one inside the ceiling is folded, not only the best-ranked one — a chain with no coordinates is a chain the next step cannot measure

That is the one chain the record reports in full. Below is every chain this step was handed, with how its own sequence was found in this genome and where it fell against the ceiling — because the ceiling is what decides whether the next step has anything to measure at all. The boundaries of a mature chain are annotated on the relative, not here, and slicing this genome at them is wrong in a way that does not announce itself: SARS-CoV's main protease starts at residue 3241 and SARS-CoV-2's at 3264, so the same cut returns 306 residues — the correct length, 23 residues into the wrong window. Each chain is aligned instead, and against every called ORF rather than only the one that matched it: a coronavirus polyprotein is read as two overlapping ORFs through a frameshift, so the polymerase and the helicase sit in a frame their own match does not point at.

Being too long is a bar to folding the chain whole, not to reaching the target. This step used to stop at the ceiling: a chain over it was abandoned, and the lane below said of those chains that nothing could be predicted for them. That was a true statement about the pipeline and a false one about the proteins. What a drug is aimed at is almost never a whole chain — it is a pocket, an interface, a catalytic site — and the relative's entry already says where those are. So each over-the-ceiling row below opens onto the functionally important regions its relative annotates, and 9 of the 9 have at least one that is short enough to fold. Papain-like protease nsp3 is the clearest case: the chain is 1,945 residues and far out of reach, and its relative's entry delimits 18 regions inside it that are not — the one offered first is CoV Nsp3 Y, at 369 residues.

Which region a row offers first is decided by two things of different standing, and the panel says which did the work on every line. The first is simply read off the entry: how many residues the curators marked as active sites, ligand-binding sites or cleavage sites fall inside the region. That is decisive for enzymes — the polymerase's catalytic domain contains six such residues against two for the domain next to it, so the annotation itself says which one matters. The second is a list of words we chose, matched against the region's description: receptor-binding, catalytic, interface and about twenty more. It exists because the first signal is silent exactly where it is needed — the spike carries one marked site residue across its whole length. It orders a list and does nothing else: no region is refused by it, and no target's rank anywhere on this page is touched by it. A region shorter than 40 residues is refused as something to fold, because coordinates for a five-residue fragment describe nothing, and it keeps its positions anyway — a short motif is exactly the kind of place a macrocycle is designed at.

What the measurement returned, chain by chain

One row for every chain on the shortlist, each answering a single question: if you wanted to put a molecule on this protein, is there anywhere on it to put one?

A patch is measured because water-repelling surface is the part of a protein this class of molecule grips. Its area is reported in square angstroms, and a macrocycle covers roughly 600 of them when it binds, which is the line drawn through every row below. The fainter line is half of it, the width under which there is not enough surface to hold a molecule at all. Every bar sits on that same scale, whether the shape underneath it was solved in a laboratory or predicted from the sequence, so the two can be read against each other.

A measured chain gets a dot and a bar. A protein is not a fixed object, and a structure is one moment of it held still, so the same chain solved twice does not give the same answer. Measuring across every structure of that domain instead of picking one moves the figure by hundreds of square angstroms. The dot is the middle of those answers and the bar runs from the smallest to the largest. Where a bar straddles the line, the verdict is uncertain however confident the dot looks, because a measurement that wide does not actually tell the two sides apart.

Nothing is missing from the list. A chain can be refused, and its row says in plain words which reason applies to it, but it cannot quietly drop out between the step and the page.

All 9 of those verdicts are decided on where the protein sits, which the gate reads before it reads area at all. Every one of these is annotated somewhere a macrocycle cannot arrive, inside the cell or inside the virion, the virus particle itself. Several of their patches clear the footprint line comfortably and are refused anyway. That is the gate saying something about this class of molecule, not about the target.

Why the second group is kept apart from the first, when the sum is the same arithmetic. A deposited structure is something that happened: a protein was purified, persuaded into a crystal or frozen in ice, put in a beam, and coordinates were fitted to what came back. A predicted structure is a claim a model makes after reading the sequence, and no one has ever looked at the molecule. Run the same patch calculation over each and you get two numbers of the same kind and two pieces of evidence that are not.

Nothing that keeps them apart is accuracy in the abstract. A deposited patch has a spread this page can show you, because the same chain has been solved several times and the structures disagree, and that disagreement is the bar. A prediction has no such bar. Ask the same model for the same sequence and it returns the same coordinates, so the error does not average out, and what the model offers instead is a confidence per residue. The main protease fold is scored 62.6 out of 100, and the stretches marked along the bottom of the figure above never clear the threshold at all, the last of them running to the end of the chain. Measuring a patch across that stretch describes a shape the model declined to commit to.

A predicted chain is also alone, folded by itself with no partner, no membrane and nothing else of the virus particle around it, so every face is out in the open, including the ones that in the real protein are pressed against something else. Greasy surface that is buried in life is exposed in the model, and the patch inflates. That is the likeliest reason the main protease's predicted patch comes out several times larger than its deposited one, as section 04 reports it. It is not separable here from ordinary prediction error, so the record states the ratio and names no cause.

The predicted group earns its place anyway, because a deposited structure exists only where somebody already did the work, and that availability tracks attention rather than importance. The two enzymes approved antivirals already hit are measured here on several structures apiece, while the membrane protein and the small accessory proteins have nothing deposited anywhere. A pipeline that can only measure depositions therefore answers best on exactly the targets that need it least, and goes quiet on a genuinely new pathogen, the case this project exists for. The prediction does not replace a measurement, and the gap between the two numbers below is the reason it cannot. What it does is turn no answer at all into an answer that says where it came from, for chains that would otherwise leave this step with a blank.

The gate reads where the protein sits before it reads area, and on a virus's replicase that is usually where the answer comes from. A patch can clear the footprint comfortably and still be refused because the protein it sits on is inside the cell, where a macrocycle cannot go. That is a statement about this class of molecule, not about the target, so it stays a gate and never a term in the ranking.
